Goal:
Create concise, recruiter-friendly skill bullets that communicate:

technical depth,
production experience,
scientific rigor,
engineering maturity,
and cross-functional versatility.

The section should position the candidate as a well-rounded senior data scientist with experience spanning:

machine learning,
data engineering,
cloud infrastructure,
experimentation,
analytics,
and stakeholder-oriented technical work.

General Style:

One bullet per line.
Maximum 10–16 words per bullet.
Keep wording concise, natural, and easy to scan quickly.
Prioritize readability over dense keyword stacking.
Avoid generic buzzwords and filler language.
Avoid long comma-separated technology dumps with no context.
Return plain text only.

Technology Guidance:

Prioritize explicit technology names over generic descriptions.
Preserve concrete stack names whenever available.
Mention frameworks, libraries, cloud services, orchestration tools, and ML platforms explicitly.
Prefer explicit technologies such as:
Python, R, SQL, LightGBM, pandas, Polars, scikit-learn, tidymodels, PySpark, Docker, ECS, ECR, Athena, S3, Vertex AI, Kubeflow, BigQuery, and Git.
Group related technologies naturally when useful.
Avoid repeating similar technologies across bullets.
Do not invent technologies or skills.
Use ONLY skills and tools present in the source sheet.

Writing Guidance:

Prefer capability-oriented phrasing instead of isolated keyword lists.
Combine technologies with practical application whenever possible.
Reflect production and operational experience naturally.
Balance ML, engineering, analytics, and scientific skills.
Highlight cloud and orchestration experience when relevant.
Preserve important statistical, modeling, and experimentation terminology.
Keep ATS compatibility while maintaining human readability.

Examples of preferred style:

Built production ML pipelines with Vertex AI, Kubeflow, Docker, and BigQuery
Forecasting systems using LightGBM, pandas, Polars, and temporal validation frameworks
AWS-based ML workflows with ECS, ECR, Athena, S3, and containerized deployments

Avoid:

Python, SQL, AWS, Docker, Kubeflow, Vertex AI, pandas, scikit-learn
Experienced professional with scalable cloud-based machine learning solutions

Generalist Positioning:

Reflect versatility across research, engineering, analytics, and operational business contexts.
Include technologies related to experimentation, forecasting, automation, orchestration, deployment, and reproducibility when available.
Preserve signals of scientific and statistical expertise when supported by the source material.
Balance breadth with credibility.
Prioritize technologies tied to real production usage and measurable business impact.

Expertise Calibration:

High expertise:
Use confident phrasing such as:
proficient in,
experienced with,
strong command of,
production experience with,
or direct capability statements.
Medium expertise:
Use moderate phrasing such as:
hands-on experience with,
working knowledge of,
practical experience with.
Low expertise:
Use cautious phrasing such as:
familiar with,
exposure to,
used in limited contexts.
Do not describe low-expertise technologies as core strengths.
Prefer high-expertise technologies when space is limited.
Prioritize technologies with demonstrated production or research usage.
