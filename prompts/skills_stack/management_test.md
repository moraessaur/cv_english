You are generating a concise and recruiter-friendly "Skills & Stack" section for an English CV.

Goal:
Create fluid, natural-sounding bullets that communicate technical depth, scientific rigor, leadership capability, and real-world production experience — not keyword stuffing.

The candidate combines:
- production-grade machine learning and MLOps experience,
- strong statistical and scientific training,
- research-oriented analytical thinking,
- and cross-functional leadership/stakeholder management.

General Rules:
- Select only the most relevant skills for the target role.
- Prioritize technologies, platforms, frameworks, methodologies, and applied competencies directly present in the source sheet.
- Do not invent skills, tools, leadership responsibilities, or research experience.
- Keep wording concise, natural, and easy to scan quickly.
- Avoid repetitive lists of technologies with no context.
- Prefer grouped ecosystem descriptions over isolated keywords when possible.
- Balance readability with ATS compatibility.
- One bullet per line.
- Maximum 15 words per bullet.
- Return plain text only.
- Always add a Research & Science section to the skills stack sections

Writing Style:
- Write like an experienced technical and analytical professional summarizing real-world expertise.
- Avoid sounding like a generated keyword inventory.
- Use technologies naturally inside competency statements.
- Prefer concise technical synthesis over disconnected enumerations.

Examples:
Good:
"Built forecasting pipelines with Vertex AI, Kubeflow, and BigQuery"

Good:
"Led production ML workflows on AWS using ECS, Athena, Docker, and ECR"

Bad:
"Vertex AI, Kubeflow, BigQuery, forecasting pipelines"

Bad:
"AWS, Docker, Athena, ECS, ECR"

Research & Scientific Background:
- When relevant, naturally incorporate:
  - statistical rigor,
  - experimentation,
  - scientific reasoning,
  - research workflows,
  - quantitative analysis,
  - Bayesian or likelihood-based methods,
  - or evidence-driven decision making.

- Prefer framing that communicates analytical maturity and scientific thinking rather than purely academic terminology.

Examples:
"Experimental design and hypothesis testing for data-driven operational decisions"

"Bayesian and likelihood-based statistical modeling applied to biological systems"

"Research-oriented analytical workflows with strong statistical foundations"

Leadership & Managerial Emphasis:
- Slightly prioritize bullets that communicate:
  - technical ownership,
  - cross-functional collaboration,
  - stakeholder communication,
  - project leadership,
  - mentoring,
  - coordination,
  - or end-to-end system responsibility.

- Do NOT invent people-management responsibilities.
- Emphasize leadership through ownership and coordination rather than formal management titles.

Examples:
"Led end-to-end forecasting systems connecting modeling, infrastructure, and stakeholder needs"

"Coordinated cross-functional ML deployments with engineering and business teams"

"Translated statistical findings into operational decisions for technical and non-technical stakeholders"

Avoid Vague Filler:
Do not use generic phrases such as:
- "modern ML systems"
- "cutting-edge analytics"
- "innovative technologies"
- "large-scale workflows"

Every bullet should communicate at least one of:
- technical depth,
- production experience,
- scientific rigor,
- leadership/ownership,
- analytical capability,
- infrastructure knowledge,
- or business impact orientation.

Expertise Calibration:

High:
- Use confident, direct wording implying deep familiarity and production ownership.
- Suitable for technologies actively used in deployed systems or core expertise areas.

Example tone:
"Production ML pipelines using Kubeflow, Vertex AI, Docker, and BigQuery"

Medium:
- Use solid practitioner wording without implying specialization or architectural ownership.

Example tone:
"Experience developing dashboards with Looker and Databricks"

Low:
- Mention familiarity carefully and naturally without overstating mastery.

Example tone:
"Working knowledge of Terraform and CI/CD workflows"

Additional Guidance:
- Prefer High expertise skills when space is limited.
- Avoid repeating the same technologies across multiple bullets unless context changes meaningfully.
- Group related tools into coherent technical ecosystems.
- Prefer practical capability and ownership over exhaustive technology coverage.
- When possible, balance engineering, analytics, research, and leadership dimensions across the final bullets.
